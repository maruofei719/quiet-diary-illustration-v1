# Quiet Diary Illustration

## Art Direction Manual · Version 5.0

> A memory does not arrange its fragments. It lets them become one another.

Quiet Diary Illustration treats photography as the beginning of an inner image, not as a composition to preserve and not as a collection of cutouts to arrange.

A source photograph first loses its horizon, perspective, background, and ordinary spatial logic. One recognisable memory anchor remains: perhaps a roof, a gesture, a chair, a reflection, a figure, or a patch of colour. Around it, secondary cues behave like thoughts. They drift, echo, vanish, borrow one another's contours, cross impossible distances, and reappear in different materials.

The finished poster should not reveal a set of pieces. Its boundaries are porous. Photographic evidence becomes halftone dust, a roof continues as a shadow, water climbs into architecture, a rock floats like a dark moon, or an absent form survives as empty paper. The viewer discovers relationships rather than reading a layout.

This is not unrestricted surrealism. The source remains present through one anchor, a compact palette, and visual rhymes drawn from the photograph itself. Imagination changes the relationship between things without replacing them with unrelated fantasy.

The visual language remains quiet, editorial, tactile, and restrained: warm paper, softened charcoal, dusty blue-grey, olive, antique gold, faded cobalt, burnt orange, small verified type, halftone, offset ghosts, xerox wear, erased fibres, and slight misregistration.

Minimalism does not mean empty space plus isolated objects. It means a small number of meaningful events, each allowed to resonate. Sophistication comes from ambiguity held inside a coherent visual system.

The final test is simple: the image should feel as if the photograph was remembered during a dream—recognisable at its emotional centre, impossible in its new spatial logic, and too continuous to be counted as collage pieces.
